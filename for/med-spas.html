Marketing for med spas and aesthetic clinics

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when med spas and aesthetic clinics advertise

Meta treats aesthetics as a sensitive category. Before and after imagery, body-focused language, and anything implying the viewer has a personal problem will get an ad rejected and can get an account restricted. Most spa ads are written in exactly that voice.

Discount-led acquisition builds the wrong list. A client acquired at seventy percent off returns at seventy percent off, and a med spa is a repeat business or it is nothing.

The consult is the product and most spas advertise the treatment instead. Selling a syringe count in an ad skips the conversation where the actual package gets sold.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for med spas and aesthetic clinics

  1. Copy and creative written against Meta's health and beauty policy from the first draft, so campaigns run instead of sitting in review, and the account stays healthy.

  2. The consultation as the offer, with a stated length, a named provider, and what the client walks out with. That is what sells the package, not a per-unit price.

  3. Treatment-specific campaigns, because injectables, laser, body contouring, and skin all have different buyers, different objections, and different seasons.

  4. Membership and package offers presented at the consult, since the entire economics of a med spa is repeat visits.

  5. Follow-up that runs for months. Aesthetic decisions are slow and private, and a lead that went quiet in March books in June if somebody stayed in touch.

04 / Questions

Questions med spas and aesthetic clinics ask

Can med spas advertise on Facebook?

Yes, inside Meta's health and beauty policy. What gets ads rejected is personal-attribute language that implies the viewer has a flaw, and before and after imagery used the wrong way. Written compliantly from the first draft, aesthetic campaigns run fine and face much less competition than the category deserves.

Why do med spa ads get rejected?

Almost always for personal attributes. Copy addressed to the viewer's body, weight, age, or skin reads as implying a personal condition, which the policy forbids. The fix is writing about the treatment and the outcome in the third person rather than accusing the reader of a problem.

How much does a med spa lead cost?

Forty to one hundred and twenty dollars for a consultation booking. That is only meaningful next to show rate and retained value. A retained client is worth thousands a year, so a hundred dollar consult lead that shows and rebooks is one of the better buys in local advertising.

Should a med spa advertise discounts?

Rarely, and never as the main engine. A client acquired at a deep discount returns expecting one, and a med spa lives on repeat visits. A first-visit incentive attached to a package or membership works. A standalone half-price treatment builds a list that never becomes profitable.

Can you use before and after photos in med spa ads?

Not the way most spas want to. Direct before and after comparisons in the ad creative are restricted under Meta's policy and are a common cause of rejections and account strikes. They belong on your own site and in the consult, where the platform's rules do not apply.
