Marketing for veterinary clinics and practices

A new veterinary client is not a visit. It is ten to fifteen years of an animal's life, which makes it one of the highest lifetime values in any local business, and almost nobody advertises like it.

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when veterinary clinics advertise

Vet practices advertise discounted vaccinations and first exams, which buys price shoppers into a category where price shoppers are the least loyal clients you can have.

The highest-value moment in the entire relationship is a new puppy or kitten, and it is one of the most targetable audiences in local advertising, and it goes almost entirely uncontested.

Wellness plans are the closest thing a practice has to recurring revenue, and most of them are never advertised at all.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for veterinary clinics

  1. New pet campaigns aimed at puppy and kitten owners, which is where a fifteen year relationship starts and where the competition is thinnest.

  2. Wellness plans as the offer rather than a discounted single visit, because the plan is what makes the client value real.

  3. Warmth and the actual team as the creative, since choosing a vet is an emotional decision about who you trust with a family member.

  4. Specialty and capability campaigns for dentistry, surgery, exotics, or emergency capacity, which differentiate you from every other clinic in town.

  5. Reminder and recall systems treated as marketing, because a lapsed client is far cheaper to recover than a new one is to buy.

04 / Questions

Questions veterinary clinics ask

Do Facebook ads work for veterinary clinics?

Yes, and the economics are unusually strong because a new client represents a decade or more of care. Choosing a vet is an emotional trust decision, which the feed handles far better than a search result, and new pet owners are one of the easiest audiences to reach.

How much should a vet clinic pay for a new client?

Forty to one hundred and fifty dollars is very reasonable against a lifetime value in the thousands. Most clinics dramatically underspend because they are measuring against the first visit instead of against the years of care that follow it.

What is the best audience for a veterinary practice?

New puppy and kitten owners. That household is choosing a vet for the next ten to fifteen years, they are choosing right now, and almost nobody is competing for their attention. It is the single most valuable and most neglected audience in the category.

Should vets advertise discounts?

Rarely. Discounted vaccinations attract price-driven clients in a category where loyalty is the entire business model. Advertise a wellness plan, a new pet package, or the practice itself instead, and you acquire the client who stays for the life of the animal.
