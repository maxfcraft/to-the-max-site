01 / The problem

What goes wrong when auto repair shops advertise

Coupon advertising buys the customer who will leave for the next coupon. An oil change special with nothing behind it is the most expensive customer in the shop.

The real prize is the customer who switches away from the dealership permanently, and that is a trust decision, not a discount decision.

Most shops never contact a customer again after the repair, which means the entire value of the relationship depends on the customer remembering them, which they do not.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for auto repair shops

  1. An entry offer tied to a real inspection, so the first visit surfaces actual work instead of just discounting a service you already do cheaply.

  2. Trust as the creative: named technicians, certifications, warranty terms, and the shop itself, because the customer is choosing who to believe.

  3. Service reminders and follow-up that keep the shop in front of the customer between visits, which is where the lifetime value actually comes from.

  4. Specialty campaigns where you have a real advantage, whether that is a brand, diagnostics, fleet work, or transmissions, since specialists beat generalists in the feed.

  5. Fleet and small business campaigns, which produce steady, scheduled, higher-margin work with no competition in the feed.

04 / Questions

Questions auto repair shops ask

Do Facebook ads work for auto repair shops?

Yes, for winning customers away from dealerships and building a local reputation, both of which are trust decisions the feed handles well. They work poorly for roadside emergencies, where the driver is searching from the side of the road and picking whoever is closest and open.

How much should an auto repair shop pay for a new customer?

Thirty to ninety dollars is reasonable when a retained customer is worth a thousand a year or more. The number that decides the campaign is not the first visit, it is the second. A first visit with no return is a customer you paid to send somewhere else.

Should an auto shop run oil change specials?

Only as a doorway to a real inspection. A standalone cheap oil change buys a customer who leaves for the next coupon. Tied to a multi-point inspection that surfaces genuine work and starts a real conversation, the same offer builds a customer who stays for years.

How do independent shops compete with dealerships?

On trust and on the relationship, not on price. Named technicians, visible certifications, a stated warranty, and honest explanations of what does and does not need doing are what move a customer off a dealership. That message works far better in a feed than in a search result.
