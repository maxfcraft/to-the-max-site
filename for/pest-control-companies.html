Marketing for pest control companies

Pest control is one of the best recurring-revenue businesses in the trades and it is almost always advertised as a one-off spray. That single framing decision is what caps most of these companies.

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when pest control companies advertise

A one-time treatment is a transaction. A quarterly plan is an annuity. Same truck, same technician, same chemical, and roughly eight times the customer value, and the ad usually sells the transaction.

Demand is seasonal and pest-specific in a way that is completely predictable, and most companies run one flat campaign all year instead of moving with it.

Termite and wildlife work carries the highest ticket in the trade and needs entirely different creative, but it gets buried inside the general pest campaign where it never gets tested.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for pest control companies

  1. The recurring plan sold as the primary offer, with the one-time treatment positioned as the more expensive way to solve the same problem.

  2. Seasonal pest campaigns that move with the calendar: ants and termites in spring, mosquitoes in summer, rodents in autumn, spiders and roaches year round.

  3. Termite inspection and wildlife removal run separately, because the ticket, the urgency, and the buyer's fear are nothing like a general pest call.

  4. Neighbourhood targeting after any real infestation event, since pest problems cluster and the street that just called you has more customers on it.

  5. Retention as the growth engine. Plan cancellations are the single biggest hole in most pest control companies and cost nothing to fix compared with new acquisition.

04 / Questions

Questions pest control companies ask

Do Facebook ads work for pest control?

Yes, especially for recurring plans and seasonal pests, where the homeowner has a visible problem and a short fuse. The campaigns that fail are the ones selling a one-time spray, because a single treatment cannot carry a lead cost the way a multi-year plan can.

How much does a pest control lead cost?

Thirty to ninety dollars in most markets. Against a two hundred dollar one-time treatment that is tight. Against a quarterly plan worth six hundred a year for several years, it is one of the cheapest customer acquisitions in local service. Advertise the plan.

Should pest control ads sell one-time treatments or plans?

Plans, with the one-time treatment offered as the more expensive alternative for the same problem. Selling the transaction caps your customer value at a single visit. Selling the plan turns identical work into recurring revenue and makes the whole advertising budget affordable.

When is the best season for pest control advertising?

It shifts by pest rather than having one peak. Ants and termites in spring, mosquitoes through summer, rodents as it cools in autumn, roaches and spiders year round. Running one unchanged campaign across all of it means being irrelevant for most of the year.

Is termite work worth advertising separately?

Yes. The ticket is far larger, the urgency is higher, and the emotional trigger is completely different from a general pest call. Buried inside a general campaign, termite creative never gets a fair budget or a fair test, which is why most companies assume it does not work.
