01 / The problem

What goes wrong when tree service and arborist companies advertise

Emergency removal after a storm is the highest-ticket work in the trade and it is won in the first hour. A company that is not already live in the ad account when the storm hits cannot get there in time.

Planned trimming and pruning is a much softer decision with a much longer window, and running it in the same campaign as emergency work confuses both.

Tree work carries the highest liability fear of any home service. Insurance and certification are the actual objection, and they are usually a footnote in the ad instead of the headline.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for tree service and arborist companies

  1. A standing storm-response campaign that can be switched on within minutes and targeted at the neighbourhoods that were actually hit.

  2. A separate planned-work campaign for trimming, pruning, and health assessments, running steadily all year.

04 / Questions

Questions tree service and arborist companies ask

Do Facebook ads work for tree service companies?

Yes, for both planned trimming and storm response, though they behave completely differently. Planned work is a steady, low-cost campaign. Storm work is a standing campaign you scale within minutes of weather, and it is the most profitable advertising in the trade when the account is already warm.

How much does a tree service lead cost?

Forty to one hundred and fifty dollars. Against an eight hundred dollar trim that is workable, and against a three thousand dollar removal it is cheap. Storm leads cost more and convert faster, because the homeowner has a tree on the garage and is not shopping around.

How do you advertise after a storm?

By scaling a campaign that was already running, targeted to the neighbourhoods that were actually hit. Building a new campaign from scratch after the storm means waiting through the learning phase while the work gets booked by somebody else. The preparation is what earns the storm revenue.

What do homeowners actually care about when hiring a tree company?

Whether you are insured and whether your crew will drop a limb through the roof. Certification, licensing, and liability coverage belong in the headline of the ad, not in fine print. Price is a distant second when a sixty foot oak is leaning over a bedroom.
