01 / The problem

What goes wrong when artists and commission studios advertise

Artists are told to build an audience and wait. That is a five year plan for a business that needs commissions this quarter.

Commissioned work is a considered, emotional, high-ticket purchase with no urgency attached to it, so without a reason to act now the lead sits forever.

Most artists have no capture mechanism at all. The work gets admired, shared, and saved, and no contact detail is ever collected.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for artists and commission studios

  1. A real offer with a boundary: a discount for the first few commissions, a seasonal deadline, or a limited number of slots, so the decision has a date on it.

  2. Lead forms that capture the subject, the occasion, and the timeline, because a commission quote is impossible without them.

  3. Portfolio-led creative using finished pieces, in the style and subject you actually want more of.

  4. Follow-up that survives a slow, emotional decision, since a commission can take weeks to commit to and the enquiry going quiet is not a no.

  5. Occasion-driven campaigns around holidays, graduations, memorials, and weddings, which is where commission demand actually concentrates.

04 / Questions

Questions artists and commission studios ask

Do Facebook ads work for artists?

Yes, for commissioned work specifically, because the decision is emotional and the imagery does the selling. What most artists lack is not reach but an offer with a deadline and a way to capture contact details. The work gets admired and saved, and nobody ever asks for a name.

How much does an art commission lead cost?

Fifteen to sixty dollars in most markets. Against a portrait commission of several hundred to a thousand dollars, that is comfortable even at a modest close rate. The constraint is usually studio capacity rather than lead cost.

What should an artist's ad offer?

Something with a boundary. A discount on the first few commissions, a limited number of slots before a holiday deadline, or a seasonal package. Commissioned art has no natural urgency, so the offer has to supply it or the enquiry sits in a drafts folder forever.

When is the best time to advertise commissioned art?

Around occasions. Christmas, Mother's Day, graduations, weddings, and memorials are when people actually commission personal work, and the lead time means the campaign has to run four to eight weeks ahead of the date, not during the week of it.
