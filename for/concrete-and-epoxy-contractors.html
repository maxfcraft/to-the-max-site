01 / The problem

What goes wrong when concrete and epoxy flooring contractors advertise

Every quote request that arrives without a budget expectation is a site visit you may not get paid for. Unqualified leads in this trade do not cost money, they cost days.

Decorative and epoxy work is highly visual and most contractors advertise it with a photo of a truck. The finished garage floor, the stamped patio, the polished showroom floor is the ad.

Commercial and industrial coating work is steadier and larger than residential, and it is almost invisible in local advertising because everyone is chasing homeowners.

04 / Questions

Questions concrete and epoxy flooring contractors ask

Do Facebook ads work for concrete and epoxy contractors?

Yes, because the product is intensely visual and the ticket is high enough to absorb a meaningful lead cost. A photograph of a finished garage floor or stamped patio in a house like the viewer's does more selling than any amount of copy about the material.

How much does a concrete or epoxy lead cost?

Eighty to two hundred and fifty dollars for a qualified lead. That is fine on a four thousand dollar garage floor. What is not fine is a cheap lead that produces an unpaid site visit, because in this trade your time is the expensive input, not the lead fee.

Should the ad include a price?

A starting price per square foot, yes. It will reduce your lead volume, which is the point. Every lead who leaves because of the number was a site visit you were never going to be paid for, and the ones who remain arrive already expecting a real conversation.

How do you qualify concrete leads before the site visit?

Ask three questions in the form: square footage, the condition of the existing surface, and when they want it done. Add a budget band if your average ticket is high. Those questions take ten seconds to answer and save entire days of driving to quotes that were never going to happen.

Is commercial epoxy work worth advertising?

It is usually the best part of the business. Warehouses, workshops, showrooms, and commercial kitchens all need coatings, the jobs are several times larger than residential, and virtually no contractor is running ads at that buyer. The auction is close to empty.
