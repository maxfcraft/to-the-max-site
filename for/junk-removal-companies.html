Marketing for junk removal and hauling companies

Junk removal is the closest thing in the trades to an impulse purchase. Somebody looks at a garage on a Saturday morning and decides today is the day, and the company that can be there this afternoon wins.

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when junk removal and hauling companies advertise

The decision is same-day and emotional, so a quote process that takes until Monday loses to anyone who answers the phone on Saturday.

Pricing is opaque in this trade, which makes customers hesitate, and hesitation in an impulse category is a lost job.

Commercial and property management work, evictions, foreclosures, construction cleanouts, office clearouts, is steadier and larger than residential and is almost never advertised to.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for junk removal and hauling companies

  1. Same-day and next-day availability as the headline, because availability is the offer in this category.

04 / Questions

Questions junk removal and hauling companies ask

Do Facebook ads work for junk removal?

Yes, and unusually well, because the trigger is visual and the decision is same-day. Somebody staring at a cluttered garage is exactly the person the feed reaches on a weekend morning. The company that can show up that afternoon takes most of that demand.

How much does a junk removal lead cost?

Twenty to sixty dollars in most markets. Against a four hundred dollar truckload that is comfortable, and against a single item pickup it is tight. Route density and minimum ticket are what make the small jobs work rather than the lead price.

Should junk removal ads show prices?

Yes, visually by load size. Opaque pricing creates hesitation, and hesitation kills an impulse purchase. A customer who can look at a graphic and estimate their own cost within fifty dollars is dramatically more likely to book than one who has to ask.

Is commercial junk removal worth advertising?

It is usually the better half of the business. Property managers, realtors handling foreclosures, and contractors doing construction cleanouts book repeatedly, at larger tickets, on a schedule. Almost nobody is advertising to them, so the auction is cheap and the accounts last for years.
