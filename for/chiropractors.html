01 / The problem

What goes wrong when chiropractic practices advertise

A cheap new patient offer produces a full calendar and an empty waiting room, because people who paid nothing feel nothing about missing an appointment.

Health is a sensitive advertising category. Copy that names the reader's pain or condition gets rejected and can put the ad account at risk, and most chiropractic copy is written exactly that way.

The practice makes money on care plans, not first visits, and most advertising stops thinking at the booking.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for chiropractic practices

  1. A paid or committed first visit rather than a free one, because a small payment is the single largest lever on show rate in this category.

  2. Copy written against Meta's health policy from the first draft, in the third person, about the care rather than the reader's body.

  3. Confirmation and reminder sequences between booking and appointment, which is where most of the no-shows are actually lost.

  4. Condition-specific campaigns where policy allows, since the message that works for a car accident is not the message that works for desk posture.

  5. Care plan conversion tracked as the real outcome, because a practice built on first visits alone is running a treadmill.

04 / Questions

Questions chiropractic practices ask

Do Facebook ads work for chiropractors?

Yes, and it remains one of the most reliable local health categories, as long as the copy is compliant and the offer produces people who actually show up. The failure is almost never traffic. It is a free offer that fills a calendar with no-shows and a practice that never tracks the difference.

Why do chiropractic ads get rejected?

Personal attributes. Copy addressed to the reader's back, neck, pain, or posture implies a personal health condition, which Meta's policy prohibits. Writing about the care and the outcome in the third person clears review reliably and performs just as well.

Should a chiropractor offer a free first visit?

No. A small paid first visit dramatically improves show rate, because a free appointment is worth nothing and gets treated accordingly. Twenty-nine to ninety-nine dollars costs you almost no lead volume and roughly doubles the number of people who actually arrive.

How much should a chiropractic new patient cost?

Forty to one hundred and twenty dollars per booked appointment, judged alongside show rate. Against a care plan worth a few thousand dollars, even the high end is comfortable. The practice that wins is the one measuring patients on plans, not bookings on a calendar.
