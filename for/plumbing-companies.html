Marketing for plumbing companies

Most plumbing marketing advice is about ranking for emergency searches. That is one narrow slice of the trade, it is the most expensive slice, and it is the one where the customer never calls you again.

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when plumbing companies advertise

Emergency intent belongs to search, and it is brutally priced. Trying to win burst pipe traffic in the Facebook feed is bidding on a moment that has not happened yet to someone who is not looking.

The profitable plumbing work is planned: water heater replacement, repipes, water treatment, sewer line, fixture upgrades during a remodel. All of it is a considered purchase with a long window, and all of it is nearly invisible in most plumbers' advertising.

Plumbers under-advertise the one thing they own that nobody else does, which is being inside the house. Every completed job is a chance at the next job and a review, and most of that value is left on the floor because nobody ever follows up.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for plumbing companies

  1. Planned-work campaigns as the core: water heaters, repipes, filtration, sewer inspection. Long consideration, real ticket, and almost nobody else in the feed.

  2. A named diagnostic offer instead of a generic free estimate, because a bounded thing you can picture books far better than a vague favour.

  3. A database reactivation pass across every past customer you already have, which on most plumbing companies pays for the first two months of advertising by itself.

  4. Review generation wired into job completion, since a plumber's local search position is largely a function of recent reviews and nothing else.

  5. Emergency campaigns kept narrow and on standby, switched on for weather events rather than run all year at full price.

04 / Questions

Questions plumbing companies ask

Do Facebook ads work for plumbers?

For planned work, yes: water heaters, repipes, filtration, sewer work, remodel fixtures. For a burst pipe at midnight, no, because that customer is on Google with a wrench in their hand. Run Meta for the considered high-ticket jobs and let search have the emergencies.

How much does a plumbing lead cost?

Forty to one hundred and fifty dollars for planned work in most markets. On a two thousand dollar water heater replacement that is comfortable math even at a one in three close rate. Emergency clicks on search cost far more and produce a customer who is unlikely to ever call you again.

What should a plumber advertise if not emergencies?

The work with a real ticket and a decision window: water heater replacement, repipes, water treatment and filtration, sewer line inspection, and fixture upgrades during remodels. These have less competition in the feed, a bigger margin, and a customer who is choosing rather than panicking.

Are reviews or ads more important for a plumbing company?

Reviews decide whether the ads work. A plumber with forty recent reviews and a plumber with four can run the same campaign and get wildly different results, because the homeowner checks before calling. Fix review generation first, then spend. It costs nothing and it changes every number downstream.

What is the fastest way for a plumber to get jobs this month?

Contact every past customer. Most plumbing companies have hundreds of people who liked the work and have not heard from them since. A short, specific message about an inspection or a water heater approaching end of life typically books work within days, before a single ad has run.
