Marketing for local and long distance moving companies

Moving is a dated purchase with a hard deadline and near-total commoditisation. The customer has one day circled on a calendar, they will get three quotes, and the first honest one usually wins.

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when moving companies advertise

Every mover looks identical in an ad: a truck, a box, a price per hour. Nothing in that creative gives a customer a reason to pick you.

Quote speed decides this category. A customer who gets a real number in ten minutes rarely waits for the company that promised a callback tomorrow.

The season is brutally compressed into summer, and companies that only advertise in season pay peak prices for jobs they were going to get anyway.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for moving companies

  1. Instant or near-instant quoting from bedroom count, distance, and date, because whoever gives a real number first usually books the job.

  2. Transparent pricing, damage policy, and insurance stated plainly, which is the actual anxiety in a trade with a reputation problem.

  3. Long distance run as a separate campaign, since the ticket, the lead time, and the buyer are completely different from a local move.

  4. Off-season campaigns for corporate relocations, senior moves, and apartment turnovers, which keep the trucks moving in winter.

  5. Specialty positioning where you have it, whether pianos, art, seniors, or offices, because a specialist is not comparable on price.

04 / Questions

Questions moving companies ask

Do Facebook ads work for moving companies?

Yes, because moves are planned weeks ahead and the audience is targetable by life event. The constraint is response speed. Movers are shopped against two or three competitors in the first hour, and the company that supplies a real number first books a disproportionate share of the work.

How much does a moving lead cost?

Thirty to ninety dollars for local and eighty to two hundred and fifty for long distance. Both work against the ticket. What breaks the maths is a slow quote, because a lead who has already booked someone else is a lead you paid full price for and never had.

How do movers stand out from every other moving ad?

By being specific about the things customers are actually afraid of: damage policy, insurance, transparent pricing, and no surprise charges on the day. The trade has a reputation problem, and a company that addresses it directly stops being comparable on hourly rate alone.

What do moving companies advertise in the off-season?

Corporate relocations, senior and downsizing moves, apartment turnovers, and storage. Summer demand arrives whether you advertise or not, and paying peak lead prices for it is the worst spend of the year. Winter is when the account should be building relationships and filling gaps.
