Marketing for landscaping and lawn care companies

There are two completely different businesses inside most landscaping companies: a recurring maintenance route, and design-build projects. They need different ads, different offers, and different definitions of a good lead.

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when landscaping and lawn care companies advertise

Advertising mowing by the cut is the fastest way to a terrible year. A one-off mow at fifty dollars cannot carry a lead cost, and the customer disappears. The product being sold has to be the season or the route, not the visit.

Design-build is where the margin is and it is sold on images. Most landscapers have hundreds of photos of finished work on their phone and run ads with none of them.

Route density decides profitability in this trade and almost nobody targets for it. Ten customers on one street is a different business than ten customers across the county, and the ad radius is what determines which one you get.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for landscaping and lawn care companies

  1. Maintenance sold as a season or a monthly plan, never as a single cut, so a lead cost can be carried by more than one visit.

  2. Tight geographic targeting built around the routes you already run, so new customers land next to existing ones and drive time stops eating the margin.

  3. A separate design-build campaign carried by your own finished-project photography, with a real starting price so the leads arrive pre-qualified.

  4. Spring pre-booking that starts in late winter, because the companies with full spring calendars filled them in February.

  5. Off-season conversion into leaf removal, cleanups, holiday lighting, and hardscape, so the crew and the ad account both stay alive through winter.

04 / Questions

Questions landscaping and lawn care companies ask

Do Facebook ads work for lawn care companies?

Yes, when you sell the season rather than the cut. A single mow cannot carry a lead cost. A monthly plan that runs for two years absolutely can, and that difference is why two lawn companies can run identical ads with completely different outcomes.

How much does a lawn care lead cost?

Twenty to sixty dollars for maintenance in most markets. That looks expensive against one mow and cheap against a customer who stays two seasons. Always measure against the lifetime of the plan, because that is what you are actually buying.

When should landscaping ads start in the spring?

Six to eight weeks before the season, which usually means late winter. Homeowners decide on lawn care before the grass starts moving, and the companies with full spring routes booked them in February. Starting in April means bidding against everyone for what is left.

How do you sell design-build landscaping with ads?

With your own finished-project photos and a stated starting price. Design-build is bought with the eyes, so stock imagery is useless. A real starting number filters out people expecting a thousand dollar backyard and leaves you with leads worth an appointment.

What do landscapers advertise in winter?

Leaf removal and cleanups, holiday lighting where you offer it, hardscape and patio projects that get planned indoors, and early booking for spring maintenance at a locked rate. Switching the ads off in November and back on in March means rebuilding the account from cold every year.
