01 / The problem

What goes wrong when window tint and paint protection shops advertise

Every tint shop in the city is running the same ad: a dark sedan, a price, and the word ceramic. When the creative is identical, the only variable left is the number, and the shop with the worst film wins the click.

The high-margin work, full-front PPF, full-vehicle wraps, ceramic coating packages, does not sell from a price ad. It sells from a specific film brand, a named warranty, and an install bay a person can see. Most shops never show any of it.

Tint has a second buyer nobody advertises to: the dealership, the fleet manager, and the home and storefront owner. Same film, same crew, much bigger ticket, almost zero competition in the feed.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for window tint and paint protection shops

  1. Creative that shows the bay, the crew, and the film brand you actually carry, because the buyer at the top of the market is buying the installer, not the tint.

  2. The offer built on the package, not the discount: film tier, warranty length, turnaround time, and a booked slot.

  3. Split campaigns by ticket size. Standard tint and full PPF are two different businesses, and running them in one campaign makes the cheap one eat the budget.

  4. A booking flow that captures the vehicle year, make, and model up front, so your quote in the reply text is a real number instead of another question.

  5. A second campaign aimed at commercial and residential flat glass, where the competition in the feed is close to nothing and the ticket is often larger.

04 / Questions

Questions window tint and paint protection shops ask

Do Facebook ads work for window tint shops?

Yes, and better than most trades, because the buyer is usually already sold on tinting and is only choosing a shop. The ad does not have to create demand. It has to show a bay, a film line, and a warranty that makes you the obvious choice, then make booking one tap.

How much does a window tint lead cost?

Twenty-five to seventy dollars in most markets, and the spread is mostly about ticket size. A standard tint lead at the low end and a PPF lead at the high end are both good buys. What breaks the math is running them in the same campaign, where the cheap leads take the budget.

Should a tint shop advertise a price or a package?

A package. Price ads in tint are a race to the bottom against shops using film that fails in two years. A named film line, a stated warranty, a turnaround time, and a photo of your own bay sells against a cheaper number, because it changes what the customer thinks they are buying.

How do you sell PPF and ceramic coating, not just cheap tint?

You run them as a separate campaign, with separate creative, aimed at newer and higher-value vehicles. PPF buyers respond to protection and resale value, not to darkness and heat rejection. Mixed into a tint campaign, PPF creative loses every auction to the cheaper offer and never gets a fair test.

Can a tint shop get commercial work from Facebook ads?

Yes, and it is the most underpriced thing in the trade. Office buildings, storefronts, and homeowners with west-facing glass all buy film, and almost nobody is advertising to them. Same crew, same film, higher ticket, and an auction with far fewer bidders in it.
