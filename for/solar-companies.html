Marketing for solar installation companies

Solar has the worst lead quality problem in local advertising. Anyone can fill in a form. Very few of those people own a suitable roof, pay a large enough bill, and can qualify for financing, and everything comes down to filtering for all three.

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when solar installation companies advertise

An unqualified solar lead is not a cheap lead, it is a negative one. It burns a setter, a closer, and a site visit on someone who was never going to be installable.

The category is saturated with door knockers, lead resellers, and exaggerated savings claims, so homeowners arrive already suspicious before you have said anything.

Incentive and rate changes move the entire market, and campaigns written around a specific credit or rate go stale overnight and keep spending.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for solar installation companies

  1. Qualification inside the form: ownership, average monthly bill, roof age and shading, and a credit band. Volume drops, closed deals go up.

  2. Honest savings framing with checkable numbers, which both protects the ad account and survives the homeowner verifying it later.

  3. Appointment setting as the conversion event, with confirmation steps aimed at getting both decision makers present.

  4. Creative built on local installs with real homeowners, because a national-looking solar ad reads as a lead reseller in a category full of them.

  5. Campaign copy reviewed whenever incentives or rates shift, so nothing keeps spending against an offer that no longer exists.

04 / Questions

Questions solar installation companies ask

Do Facebook ads work for solar companies?

Yes, but only with hard qualification built into the form. Solar generates lead volume effortlessly and most of it is unusable, because the homeowner rents, has a shaded or failing roof, or cannot finance. Filtered properly, Meta is one of the better solar channels. Unfiltered, it will quietly drain a budget.

How much does a solar lead cost?

Raw leads can be cheap and are usually worthless. A qualified appointment costs two hundred to six hundred dollars, and on a twenty-five thousand dollar install closing at one in four sits, that is very workable. Never judge a solar campaign on cost per lead.

How do you qualify solar leads?

Four questions: do they own the home, what is the average monthly electricity bill, how old is the roof and is it shaded, and roughly what credit band are they in. Each one cuts volume and raises close rate, and the combination is the difference between a profitable campaign and a treadmill.

Why is solar lead quality so bad?

Because the offer is attractive to almost everyone and installable for a minority. The category is also crowded with resellers sending the same lead to several companies. If your form does not filter, you are buying the same unqualified homeowner that four competitors already called this morning.
