01 / The problem

What goes wrong when auto detailing shops advertise

A gorgeous reel with no offer is entertainment. It gets saved, it gets shared, and it books nothing, because the viewer was never told what to do or what it costs.

Detailers train their own customers to wait for a discount. Run a half-price special every month and the full-price package stops existing, because the market learns your real price is the sale price.

The ceramic coating customer and the twenty dollar wash customer are not the same person and do not respond to the same ad. Most shops run one campaign at both and get the cheap one.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for auto detailing shops

  1. Three named packages with real prices, so the ad has something to close on instead of ending at a direct message.

  2. Coating and correction run as their own campaign into higher-value vehicle audiences, kept away from the entry-level wash creative that would eat the budget.

  3. Booking straight into a calendar slot, not a conversation. Every message thread between the ad and the appointment is a place the customer leaves.

  4. A maintenance plan offered at pickup, because a detailed car needs upkeep and a recurring customer is worth ten times the one-off.

  5. Before and after on cars that look like the target customer's car. A corrected supercar sells nothing to the person driving a three-year-old SUV.

04 / Questions

Questions auto detailing shops ask

Do Facebook ads work for auto detailing?

Yes, because the results are visual and the before and after does the selling for you. What stops most detailers is that the ad ends in a conversation instead of a booking. Attach a named package, a real price, and a calendar link, and the same creative starts producing appointments.

How much does an auto detailing lead cost?

Fifteen to forty-five dollars for standard detailing and fifty to one hundred and fifty for ceramic coating work. The coating lead looks expensive until you put it against a fifteen hundred dollar ticket, at which point it is the best buy in the shop.

Should detailers run discounts?

Sparingly, and never on a schedule. A monthly special teaches the market that your real price is the discount, and the full-price package quietly dies. Use a bundle, a free add-on, or a limited number of slots at a named date instead. Same urgency, no damage to the price.

How do you sell ceramic coating with ads?

As a separate campaign with its own creative, aimed at newer and higher-value vehicles, selling protection and resale value rather than shine. Coating creative mixed into a wash campaign loses every auction to the cheaper offer, so it never gets a real test and the shop concludes coating does not sell.

Is mobile detailing harder to advertise than a shop?

It is different math, not harder. Mobile has no location to draw people to, so the targeting radius and drive time have to be priced into every job before you decide what a lead can cost. Tight radius, higher minimum ticket, and route-clustered scheduling are what make mobile work.
