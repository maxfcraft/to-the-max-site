Marketing for salons and barbershops

A salon does not have a traffic problem, it has a rebooking problem. A client who comes eight times a year is worth ten of a client who came once on a discount and never returned.

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when salons and barbershops advertise

Discount-led acquisition is endemic in this category and it buys exactly the client who leaves for the next discount, in a business that only works on repeats.

The high-ticket services, colour, extensions, treatments, keratin, are where the margin is, and the ads are usually about haircuts.

Stylists build personal followings and salons advertise the building, which wastes the strongest asset in the shop.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for salons and barbershops

  1. A first-visit offer tied to a rebooking, so the discount buys a relationship instead of a single appointment.

  2. High-ticket service campaigns run separately, because a colour correction client and a fifteen dollar cut client are not the same buyer.

  3. Stylist-led creative using real work from real chairs, with permission, since clients follow people and not premises.

  4. Online booking straight from the ad, because a direct message thread is where salon bookings go to die.

  5. Rebooking and recall built into the close of every appointment, which is worth more than any campaign in this category.

04 / Questions

Questions salons and barbershops ask

Do Facebook and Instagram ads work for salons?

Yes, and Instagram in particular, because the work is visual and the decision is local and personal. The reason salon ads disappoint is rarely the traffic. It is that the new client never rebooks, which turns every acquisition into a one-time transaction.

How much should a salon pay for a new client?

Fifteen to fifty dollars, which is cheap against a client worth several hundred to a couple of thousand a year. That number is only good if rebooking works. Without it you are paying repeatedly for first visits that go nowhere.

Should a salon run discount offers?

Only when the discount is attached to a rebooking or a package. A standalone half-price cut attracts the client who chases the next half-price cut elsewhere. Tie the offer to a second appointment and the same spend buys a relationship instead of a transaction.

Should ads feature the salon or the stylists?

The stylists. Clients follow people, not premises, and a stylist's own work is the most persuasive creative available to the shop. Feature real work from real chairs with permission, and let each stylist's following do the work the building never could.
