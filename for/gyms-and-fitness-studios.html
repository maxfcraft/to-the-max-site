Marketing for gyms and fitness studios

A gym is a subscription business wearing a building. Every advertising decision should be made against what a member is worth over their whole stay, and almost none of them are.

01 / The problem

What goes wrong when gyms and fitness studios advertise

Free trial campaigns produce enormous lead volume and terrible members. Something free is worth nothing and gets treated accordingly, which is why the show rate on free trials is dismal.

The January rush is a trap. A gym that acquires its whole year in six weeks has a churn cliff in March and no plan for it.

Almost every gym measures cost per lead and stops there, which hides the only number that matters: what it costs to acquire a member who is still there in month six.

02 / The build

What To The Max builds for gyms and fitness studios

  1. A paid trial or challenge instead of a free one. A small amount of money changes the show rate more than any other single lever in this category.

  2. Follow-up built for a decision people make with their ego. Speed matters, but so does a sequence that survives the week they decided to start Monday and did not.

  3. Offers matched to your actual model. A twenty-four hour access gym, a boutique studio, and a personal training business are three different businesses and cannot share one campaign.

  4. Retention treated as marketing. A member who stays nine months instead of four doubles what you can afford to pay for the next one.

  5. Community and results content as the creative, using real members with permission, because a stranger deciding on a gym is deciding whether they will fit in there.

04 / Questions

Questions gyms and fitness studios ask

Do Facebook ads work for gyms?

Yes, and gyms are one of the strongest local categories for it, because the decision is emotional, visual, and geographically bounded. The reason gym campaigns fail is almost never targeting. It is a free offer that produces leads who never show up and a follow-up process that stops after one call.

How much should a gym pay per member?

One to two months of dues is a healthy acquisition cost, which at a hundred dollar membership means one to two hundred dollars per joined member. Cost per lead is a distraction. Measure cost per member still paying in month six, because that is the person who actually funds the gym.

Are free trials or paid trials better for gym ads?

Paid, almost always. A free trial produces far more leads and far fewer members, because something free is worth nothing and gets treated that way. A small paid commitment, even twenty or thirty dollars, transforms the show rate and filters for people who actually intend to start.

When should a gym advertise?

All year, with the budget weighted toward January and September but never concentrated there. A gym that acquires its entire year in the January rush faces a churn cliff in March with no pipeline behind it. Steady acquisition with seasonal peaks beats a single annual spike.

What should a gym advertise, classes or membership?

Whichever one matches how you actually make money. A boutique studio sells the class experience and a specific instructor. A twenty-four hour access gym sells convenience and price. A personal training business sells a transformation and a person. Running one campaign for all three is why most gym ads say nothing.
