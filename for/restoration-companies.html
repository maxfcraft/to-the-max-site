01 / The problem

What goes wrong when water, fire, and mold restoration companies advertise

True emergency intent belongs to search and to the phone, not to the feed. A homeowner standing in water is not scrolling.

Referral partners, plumbers, insurance agents, property managers, realtors, drive most of the volume in this trade, and almost nobody advertises to them.

Demand is event-driven and unpredictable, and an ad account that has been switched off for two months cannot be scaled in the twelve hours that matter.

04 / Questions

Questions water, fire, and mold restoration companies ask

Do Facebook ads work for restoration companies?

Partly. They work well for referral partner acquisition, brand presence before an event, and storm response scaled the day weather hits. They work poorly for the emergency itself, because a homeowner with water rising is calling, not scrolling. The feed builds the pipeline, the phone catches the emergency.

How do restoration companies get more referrals?

By advertising to the referrers. Plumbers, insurance agents, property managers, and realtors decide where a large share of restoration work goes, and almost nobody runs campaigns at them. It is the cheapest and least contested audience in the entire category.

How much does a restoration lead cost?

One hundred to four hundred dollars for a qualified consumer lead. Against a five thousand dollar mitigation job that is fine, and against a large loss it is trivial. The real bargain is a referral partner who sends several jobs a year for a fraction of that.

How do you prepare for storm season?

By keeping the account warm year round. A campaign that has been running steadily can be scaled within an hour of an event. One built from scratch after the storm spends its first days in the learning phase while every job in the market gets booked by someone else.
