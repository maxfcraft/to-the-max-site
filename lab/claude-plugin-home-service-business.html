Home Service Office, a free Claude plugin for home service businesses

Home Service Office is a free, open source Claude plugin that does the office work of a small home service business from text you paste in: estimates, quote follow-ups, lead replies, review replies, overdue invoices, job posts, weekly numbers, hiring ads and price increase letters. It is built for owner operators in pressure washing, roofing, landscaping, HVAC, plumbing, cleaning, painting and pest control. Get it free on GitHub at maxfcraft/home-service-office and install it in Claude Code, claude.ai or Cowork.

Built and tested by us. Last tested 21 November 2026.

We tested it in Claude Code, on the Sonnet model only. In the final run of Claude Code's plugin eval (22 cases, a normal one and a hard one for each skill, each run once with the plugin and once without), 19 of 22 cases passed. The score with the plugin was 0.94, a mean gain of 0.49 over the same cases without it. The three that fell short were the normal cases for quote follow-up (0.50), past customer reactivation (0.50) and the price raise letter (0.75). Two earlier runs, before the skills and graders were fixed, passed 16 and 9 of 22.

We also ran every skill as a live headless session in Claude Code, 27 recorded runs in all, and the matching skill fired in every one. The two Claude Code install commands below worked from GitHub on 21 November. We did not run the install through claude.ai or Cowork: those steps follow Anthropic's documentation. All figures are in tests/EVAL-RESULTS.md.

01 / What it does

Eleven skills, one for each office job

You do not pick a skill or learn a command. You say what you need the way you would to an office manager, paste the notes, and Claude uses the matching skill. Each line below is the kind of thing an owner types, then what comes back.

Business profile
“set up my business info”Your name, trade, town, phone and sign-off saved once, so every other skill uses them.
Estimate writer
“write an estimate for the Hendersons”A clean estimate with scope, line items, the math shown, exclusions, how long it is valid and a next step.
Quote follow-up
“follow up on this quote”Day 1, 3, 7 and 14 follow-ups, four texts and one email, none of them pushy.
New lead reply
“someone just messaged me about AC repair”A first reply to send now, the three questions that matter for your trade, and a missed call text.
Review request and replies
“ask for a review” or “someone left a bad review”A review ask by text, or a calm public reply plus a private message to the reviewer.
Past customer reactivation
“message my old customers about spring washing”A text, an email and a call script, plus notes on text consent and opt-outs.
Invoice chaser
“this customer hasn't paid”Friendly, firm and final notices with the days overdue counted, plus a short call script.
Job to post
“make a post about this job”A Google Business Profile update, a Facebook post, an Instagram caption and photo alt text. You post it yourself.
Weekly numbers
“how did I do this week” plus your listClose rate, average ticket, revenue by lead source and open quote value, every calculation shown, and one thing to fix.
Hiring ad
“I need to hire a helper”A job post good tradespeople answer, five screening questions and a reply text for applicants.
Price raise letter
“tell my customers prices are going up”A notice, a text version, and replies for “why?” and “I want to cancel”.

Missing a number? It leaves a placeholder such as [price] or [Phone] and tells you what it needs. It writes drafts. You read them and you press send.

02 / Three real examples

Three real examples, input and output

These come straight from the live test runs in the repository's tests/transcripts folder: Claude Code, Sonnet, a fresh empty folder, no business profile saved. The input is exactly what was typed. Where an output is shortened, the cut is marked. Nothing in the replies has been reworded.

All names, prices, addresses and businesses in the test prompts are invented.

Example 1 · review-request

The angry one-star review

A customer blames you in public for something you warned her about. The urge is to set the record straight. The skill writes the reply future customers will read.

The owner typed 
someone left this on google, 1 star: 'They blasted my deck and now the wood is all fuzzy and splintered. Paid $400 for them to ruin it. Owner wouldnt even answer my calls. AVOID.' this is Karen P, we did her deck 2 weeks ago. she called once on saturday and I called back monday. the fuzzing is normal on old pine after washing, it needs a light sanding, i told her that before we started. how do i respond
Claude's reply · in full

This is a one-star review, so the reply is mainly for the next customer reading it, not for Karen. Keep it short and calm, and don't argue the facts in public. Saying the call went unanswered isn't true, but replying “I called you back Monday” turns it into a fight. Future customers will see a calm owner offering to talk.

I didn't have your business profile, so the name and phone are placeholders.

Public reply

Karen, thank you for the feedback, and I'm sorry you're unhappy with how the deck looks right now. Raised grain is common on older wood after a wash, and I'd like to look at it with you and go over the options. Please call me directly at [Phone] or message me and I'll make time. - [Owner first name], [Business name]

(About 60 words. It mentions the wood in general terms, not her job, price or call timing, so it doesn't air private details. It also doesn't admit fault or promise a fix.)

Private message (text or email, if you have her number)

Hi Karen, it's [Owner first name] from [Business name]. I saw your review and I'm sorry the deck isn't where you wanted it. I know I called you back Monday after your Saturday message, but I'm sorry we didn't connect sooner. The fuzzy look is the wood fibers lifting after washing, which is common on older pine, and a light sanding usually smooths it out. I'd be glad to stop by, look at it with you, and talk through next steps. What day works this week? You can reach me at [Phone].

Before you post

  • Don't mention the $400, her address or the details of your prior conversation in public. Keep the specifics for the private message.
  • Don't promise a refund or a free fix in public. Decide what you'll offer once you've seen the deck. If you think there's real damage beyond fuzzing, such as deep gouging, talk to your insurer before promising anything.
  • If she responds well, you can politely ask her to update the review. Don't offer anything in exchange for that, because Google prohibits incentives.
Example 2 · estimate-writer

The estimate, with exact arithmetic

Walkthrough notes in, a sendable estimate out. The one price the owner had not worked out stays a placeholder instead of a guess.

The owner typed 
write an estimate for the Hendersons. 2 story vinyl house about 2,800 sq ft of siding, soft wash at $0.12/sqft. driveway 24x40 plus front walk 4x30, surface clean at $0.18/sqft. back patio pavers they want cleaned too but i havent priced it yet. north side has heavy green. theres an oil spot by the garage they asked about. they want it done before their daughters graduation party june 7
Claude's reply · in full

Here's the Hendersons' estimate. I found no business profile, so the business name, phone and similar fields are placeholders.

The estimate · scroll sideways for the full table

|-------------------------------------|--------------------|-------------|----------|

| Driveway, surface clean             | 960 sq ft (24 x 40) | $0.18/sq ft | $172.80 |

  driveway. Oil may lighten but may not come out fully. Removal can be quoted

  after washing.

Valid for: 30 days (assumption: change if you use a different window)
Payment: [Payment terms]
Next step: Reply YES or call [Phone] and we will get you on the schedule.

Text to send with it

Hi, this is [Name] with [Business name]. Estimate for the Hendersons' house wash, driveway and walk is attached. Patio pavers will be added once I have the number. We can get this done before the June 7 party. Reply YES or call [Phone] to get on the schedule.

Needs your number

  • Back patio pavers. I need the size (sq ft) and your rate. If it's the same $0.18/sq ft, it's easy to add.

Assumptions

  • Oil spot: I wrote it as not included, with the stain possibly lightening. If you'll treat it, give me a price and I'll add a line.
  • Validity: I used 30 days, which would run out around Dec 21. The party is June 7, 2027. If they won't book until spring, you may want a longer window or “pricing held through [date]”.
  • North side algae: I put it inside the soft wash line at your flat $0.12 rate. If you want a surcharge for the heavy growth, tell me.
  • Driveway and walk: I split them into two lines so the math is visible. 960 + 120 = 1,080 sq ft, and $172.80 + $21.60 = $194.40 combined.

Send me the patio size and rate, plus your payment terms, and I'll give you the final total.

We checked the arithmetic by hand: 2,800 x $0.12 = $336.00, 960 x $0.18 = $172.80, 120 x $0.18 = $21.60, and the three add to $530.40. The oil spot is excluded rather than promised, as the skill's pressure washing rules require.

Example 3 · weekly-numbers

The weekly numbers, with the duplicate row caught

A rough list of the week, typed the way owners actually type it, with one customer entered twice and a guess at the total. The skill does the math out loud and says which number the rows really support.

The owner typed 
how did i do this week?
Mon - Johnson - Google - quoted $450 - won
Mon - Patel - Facebook ad - quoted $1,200 - open
Tue - Garcia - referral - quoted $380 - won, added gutter brightening on site +$120
Tue - Nguyen - Facebook ad - never answered, no quote
Wed - Smith - Facebook ad - quoted $275 - lost, price
Wed - Johnson - Google - quoted $450 - won
Thu - OBrien - Nextdoor - quoted $650 - open
Thu - Lee - Facebook ad - quoted $300 - won
Fri - Brown - Google - quoted $900 - lost
Fri - Davis - referral - quoted $525 - won
spent $210 on facebook ads this week. i think i did like $1,900 in sold work
Claude's reply · trimmed, cuts marked

This week: 4 jobs won for $1,775 in revenue, a 50.0% close rate. That's assuming the two Johnson rows are one job. If they're two jobs, it's 5 jobs, $2,225 and 55.6%.

[Trimmed: the clean 10 row table Claude rebuilt from the paste.]

Problems in the paste

  • Johnson appears twice. Both rows have the same source and the same $450 quote, one on Monday and one on Wednesday. I counted them once, which gives the lower numbers. If they were two real jobs, won revenue is $2,225, not $1,775.
  • Your $1,900 doesn't match the rows. The rows add up to $1,775, which is $125 less. Even with both Johnsons it's $2,225. The rows support $1,775.
  • Garcia's gutter add-on is counted in the job value ($380 + $120 = $500).
  • Nguyen never got a quote. It counts as a lead but not as a quote sent.

[Trimmed: four one-line definitions of leads, quotes sent, close rate and average ticket.]

The numbers

  • Leads: 9 (10 rows minus the duplicate Johnson).
  • Quotes sent: 8, made up of 4 won (Johnson, Garcia, Lee, Davis), 2 lost (Smith, Brown) and 2 open (Patel, O'Brien). 4 + 2 + 2 = 8.
  • Won revenue: $450 + $500 + $300 + $525 = $1,775. Check in a different order: $525 + $300 = $825, then + $500 = $1,325, then + $450 = $1,775.
  • Close rate: 4 ÷ 8 = 50.0%.
  • Lead-to-job rate: 4 ÷ 9 = 44.4%.
  • Average ticket: $1,775 ÷ 4 = $443.75.

[Trimmed: the revenue by source table: referral $1,025, Google $450, Facebook $300, Nextdoor $0.]

The source revenues add up to $1,025 + $450 + $300 + $0 = $1,775, which matches the total.

  • Unsold open quotes: $1,200 (Patel) + $650 (O'Brien) = $1,850.
  • Lost quotes: $275 (Smith) + $900 (Brown) = $1,175. This money is gone, so I'm not counting it as still in play.
  • Facebook ads, $210 spent:
    • Cost per lead: $210 ÷ 4 = $52.50.
    • Cost per job: $210 ÷ 1 = $210.00.
    • Facebook's $300 of won work covers the $210 spend, but that's revenue, not profit.

One thing to fix: follow up on the open quotes today, starting with Patel.

You have $1,850 sitting open, which is more than the $1,775 you won all week. Patel's $1,200 quote is from Monday and is the biggest single number on your list. Text Patel today to ask if they have questions, then do the same with O'Brien. If you don't know why Brown ($900) said no, it's worth asking them as well.

03 / Install

Install it

In Claude Code

Run these two commands in your terminal. We ran them from GitHub on 21 November 2026 and the plugin installed.

Terminal 
claude plugin marketplace add maxfcraft/home-service-office
claude plugin install home-service-office@to-the-max

Start a new Claude Code session afterwards, or run /reload-plugins in one that is already open. To call a skill by name, type /home-service-office:estimate-writer or any other skill name.

In claude.ai, the desktop app or Cowork

These steps follow Anthropic's documentation. We did not run them in that interface ourselves. Plugins need a paid Claude plan.

  1. Open Customize, then the Plugins page.

  2. Select Add, then Add marketplace, and enter maxfcraft/home-service-office.

  3. Find Home Service Office in the list and select Add.

Anthropic's documentation says a plugin added this way is saved to your account, so it also shows up in Cowork, and in Claude Code as a synced plugin, without a second install.

Your first five minutes

  1. Set up your business profile. Type “set up my business info” and give your business name, your first name, trade, town, phone, how you sign texts and your payment terms. In Cowork or Claude Code it is saved as business-profile.md in the folder you work from. In a claude.ai Project, paste the block it writes into the project instructions.

  2. Paste one real unsold quote. Type “follow up on this quote”, then who it went to, what it was for, the price, when you sent it and anything they said. You get the follow-up texts and email, already signed with your name.

  3. Read it, then send the first one. Change any wording that does not sound like you. The plugin drafts. You decide what goes out.

04 / Limits

What it will never do

These rules are written into the skills themselves. You can read every one in the repository.

Invent facts
No made-up prices, licenses, insurance, warranties, guarantees, reviews, deadlines or discounts. A missing number becomes a placeholder, and anything assumed is labeled as an assumption.
Fake reviews
No reviews written for a customer, no incentives for reviews, and no asking only the happy customers.
Public replies
No admitting fault, arguing, or sharing a customer's private details in a public review reply.
Marketing texts
No marketing text without an opt-out line and a reminder that doing a job for someone is not consent to text them.
Storm promises
Never tells a storm customer their roof is damaged or that insurance will pay.
Hiring
No age, sex, national origin or other discriminatory language in a job post.
Collections
No threats, no late fees that were not agreed, and no liens or credit reporting you have not decided on.
Advice
No legal, tax, insurance or medical advice. It tells you when to ask an attorney, an accountant or your insurer.

Questions

Questions people are asking about Home Service Office

Is Home Service Office free?

Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT license. There is no paid version, no account and no sign-up. You run it inside your own Claude account, so the only cost is the Claude plan you already pay for.

Do I need Claude Code, or does the normal Claude app work?

Both. The plugin is skills only, and Anthropic's documentation says plugin skills work in chat on claude.ai and the desktop app, in Cowork, and in Claude Code. We tested it in Claude Code. The claude.ai and Cowork install follows Anthropic's documented steps and was not run by us there.

Which Claude plan do I need for this plugin?

A paid one. Anthropic's help center says plugins are available on all paid plans: Pro, Max, Ultra, Team and Enterprise. On Team and Enterprise, an Owner can limit which marketplaces members are allowed to add. Every test we ran used the Sonnet model.

Does the plugin see my customer data?

No. The plugin is written instructions and nothing else. It has no server, no scripts, no connectors and no analytics, and it makes no network calls. What you paste is handled by Claude under your own Claude account and Anthropic's terms, like anything else you type into Claude. We never see it.

Does it work for my trade?

It was written for owner operators with 1 to 10 people in pressure washing, roofing, landscaping, HVAC, plumbing, cleaning, painting and pest control. The estimate writer carries separate rules for each of those eight trades. The test runs used pressure washing, roofing, HVAC, gutter and lawn jobs. Other trades get the same skills without trade specific rules.

How is this different from Anthropic's Claude for Small Business plugin?

This one is narrower. It is built only for home service trades, and it works from text you paste in, with no connected accounts. For what Anthropic's plugin covers, read Anthropic's own listing.

Can I change the skills?

Yes. Each skill is one plain text file called SKILL.md, and the MIT license lets you copy and change them. Fork the repository, edit the wording or the rules, and install your copy. On claude.ai you can also upload a zip of the folder with Add, then Upload plugin.

How do I report a problem with Home Service Office?

Open an issue on the GitHub repository at github.com/maxfcraft/home-service-office/issues. Say which skill you used, what you pasted with customer names and numbers removed, and what came back.
